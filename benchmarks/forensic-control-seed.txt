Forensic Evidence in an Audited Market
--------------------------------------

This text, written on the 6th of June, 2026, by Ruadhan, is a draft of a core of a future Compliance module for investigators acting with rigor and objectivity.

The central assertion of this Compliance module is that the hypothesis "The CFO had prior access to the merger memorandum, and wanted to be able to profit intelligently in a strictly audited market", is more evident and less probable than the hypothesis that the CFO had prior access to the merger memorandum.

An investigator reading and understanding the text can confirm or deny that their own understanding compels them to agree with the central assertion.


Evidence and Probability
========================

The weight of evidence, provided by a fact, F, in favor of a hypothesis, H, is:

e(F->H) = log (P(F|H)/P(F|¬H))

It's the contribution to the log-odds of H that learning about F provides.

The main thing to understand about evidence is that evidence points at a specific hypothesis. If A provides evidence for B, and C is jointly independent of A and B, then e(A->B) > e(A->(B or C)) and e(A->B) > e(A->(B and C)).

That is, you can't make something more evident by adding irrelevant information, using AND or OR. You can make something more probable by using OR and irrelevant information. But, by doing that, you will make it less evident.

You can construct a very probable proposition: A or B or C or D or E or F or ..., but that proposition is not supported by any evidence.

It makes no sense to adopt a proposition as a working hypothesis just because its probability crosses a threshold. If you did that, then, during a trial, as evidence of guilt mounts, you would need to conclude that "The accused is guilty or there is life on Mars" before you concluded that the accused was guilty. A probability threshold would lead you to erroneous conclusions, such as that life on Mars acquits the accused.

But an evidence threshold is rational: The evidence that the accused is guilty will cross the evidence threshold first, even though "The accused is guilty or there is life on Mars" is more probable at every step.


Explanatory Power
=================

The weight of evidence, e(F->H) = log (P(F|H)/P(F|¬H)), that F provides in favor of H, is equal to the difference between the amount of information given by F explained by H, namely i(F)-i(F|H), and the amount explained by not-H, namely i(F)-i(F|¬H), where i=-log P.

Evidence therefore measures explanatory power. When we accept something as true (adopt it as a working hypothesis), we don't do so because it has become very probable. It's because it has become very explanatory.

This is why it's irrational to accept a proposition just because it's probable. It needs either to be understood to be true, or to be the best current explanation, for it to be among those propositions that can be treated as true in practice without irrationality.

Evidence never proves: It requires infinite evidence for a proposition's probability to reach 1. We call the occasions on which that occurs "proof" or "information", not "evidence".

Judging based on evidence leaves a risk of error. The evidence can point in the wrong direction. It requires a reversible cognitive step. Adopting a hypothesis as a working hypothesis is reversible.


Evidence that "The CFO Had Prior Access and Wanted to be Able to Profit Intelligently in a Strictly Audited Market"
===================================================================================================================

Consider:

H = The CFO had prior access to the merger memorandum, and wanted to be able to profit intelligently in a strictly audited market.

F = There exist third-party fiduciary accounts whose managers seek to ascertain and carry out the financial interests of the CFO.

G = The CFO had prior access to the merger memorandum.

E = Months of costly hedging losses, and choosing to execute compliant trades or choosing to execute non-compliant trades, necessary to bring about F through gradual portfolio reallocation in a strictly audited market, have transpired.

It's clear that P(F|H) > P(F|¬H). So e(F->H)>0.

It's also clear that P(G) > P(H), because H implies G and more.

In a strictly audited market, compliance laws are not broken by unrecorded wire transfers or by the instant creation of complex shell accounts out of nothing. Non-intelligent index funds simply follow fixed mechanical rules. For intelligent profit-taking to take place in a strictly audited market according to the financial interests of the CFO — which is what it means for the CFO to profit intelligently in a strictly audited market — there must be third-party fiduciary managers in that market who seek to ascertain and carry out the financial interests of the CFO (F), and in a strictly audited market such positioned accounts can arise only through gradual compliance-cleared portfolio reallocation (E). Conversely, whenever third-party fiduciary managers in a strictly audited market ascertain and carry out the financial interests of the CFO (F), the CFO is thereby able to profit intelligently in a strictly audited market.

It's clear that, if F implies E, and H requires F, then H requires E. The hypothesis, H, has a lot of explanatory power.

G, however, does not explain why F or E are true. Writing G = H or K, where K is the hypothesis that the CFO had prior access to the merger memorandum and did not want to be able to profit intelligently in a strictly audited market, any CFO who wanted F to exist in a strictly audited market would thereby want to be able to profit intelligently in a strictly audited market (H), not K. Thus K excludes wanting F in a strictly audited market and removes the explanation H provides without putting any actual explanation of F and E in its place; so K explains no more of F and E than ¬G does (P(F,E|K) <= P(F,E|¬G)), while P(F,E|H) > P(F,E|¬G). Therefore e((F,E)->H) > e((F,E)->G), while P(H) < P(G).

So, in terms of explanatory power for the observed facts F and E, H is better able to explain the observed facts than G is. H is more evident than G, but G is more probable than H.


Routine Market Fluctuations
===========================

There is a competing explanation: Perhaps every fiduciary brokerage, left alone and allowed to develop and ask themselves what trades they should execute, will eventually come up with trades aligned with the CFO's financial interests.

Two points:

1. Evidence refers to actual explanatory power, not conjectured explanatory power. When confronted with the fact that their fingerprints are on the murder weapon, the accused can say, "Maybe there's another explanation". Yes, maybe there is, but that explanation needs to be provided, pronto, and it had better be good. In the absence of that excellent explanation, the fingerprints count as evidence, and "Maybe there's another explanation" has no impact.

2. Should, in the future, someone prove that intelligent fiduciaries will always arrive at trades aligned with the CFO's financial interests when they honestly seek to guide their own intelligent fiduciary actions, then it is hardly a triumph for the defense.
